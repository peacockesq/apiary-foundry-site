01

The company

UpgradedPoints is a travel and credit card affiliate publisher. The business model is straightforward: create expert content about travel rewards, credit cards, and loyalty programs; earn affiliate commissions when readers apply for recommended cards through the site"s links.

The founder was a world-class SEO expert. The organic traffic engine was sophisticated and successful. The paid media side was underdeveloped and lagging the organic opportunity.

02

The problem

UpgradedPoints was not losing money on paid media. It was simply not using paid media as a scalable growth channel. The founder knew SEO inside and out; the paid side lacked the measurement infrastructure to make budget decisions with confidence.

The specific gaps:

  • No conversion value routing back to Google Ads — the algorithm could not optimize for actual revenue
  • No click ID retention across the customer journey — the connection between ad click and card approval was lost
  • No systematic way to test whether paid traffic learnings could improve organic content strategy
  • No framework for scaling from "working experiment" to "primary revenue channel"

The founder needed someone who understood both paid media economics and the measurement layer that makes those economics trustworthy.

03

The build: in-house middleware in two months

Willie joined as the paid media lead and immediately identified the tracking gap as the highest-leverage fix. Instead of buying an external tool, he worked with the founder and an internal developer to build a RedTrack-equivalent in-house.

The architecture:

  1. Capture click IDs from Google Ads at arrival
  2. Store them in a database tied to session and user identifiers
  3. Match conversions (card applications, approvals) to the original ad click
  4. Send actual conversion values back to Google Ads through the API
  5. Build reporting that showed revenue per keyword, per ad group, per campaign

Two months of build time. The internal developer was exceptional. The founder understood the SEO implications immediately. Once the data was flowing, the system became self-improving: the algorithm learned which keywords produced high-value card applications, and budget shifted automatically toward those signals.

04

The results

The account went profitable immediately after the middleware launch. And it stayed profitable.

  • 43 consecutive profitable months without a single losing month
  • Maxed out Google Ads — scaled to the point where additional search impression share was not available at profitable economics
  • Moved to Meta Ads — extended the model to paid social with a 1.1–1.2x ROAS, slightly lower than search but still strongly profitable
  • Scaled past $1 million per month in profitable ad spend at peak
  • Ran like clockwork — the last six to seven months before departure were consistently profitable without heroic interventions

The system worked because the infrastructure was correct. Once click IDs, conversion values, and revenue signals were flowing back to the platforms, the machine learning did what it is designed to do: find more of the traffic that converts, and less of the traffic that does not.

05

The SEO / paid synergy

The most underrated outcome was the feedback loop between paid and organic.

Google Ads tests keyword demand in real time. SEO takes months to show results. By running paid campaigns on emerging topics, the UpgradedPoints team could identify high-intent search themes before investing months of editorial work.

The reverse was also true: high-performing organic content identified topics where paid competition was lower than expected, creating arbitrage opportunities.

That synergy only works when both channels share a measurement foundation. If paid and organic use different attribution models, different click ID retention policies, and different conversion definitions, the feedback loop becomes noise. The middleware Willie built unified the data layer so both channels could learn from each other.

06

What happened after departure

Willie left when the company decided to relocate the entire marketing team. He declined to move. The departure was respectful — no bridges burned, no drama.

The 43-month streak ended shortly after. The new administration lost money for five consecutive months on paid search. The same infrastructure was in place. The operator who understood how to maintain it, question it, and adjust it was not.

That is not a knock on the replacement team. It is a statement about what kind of work this is. A measurement system is not a set-it-and-forget-it installation. It needs someone who understands the economics, watches the data, and makes the hard calls about where budget should move.
